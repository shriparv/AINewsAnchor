📰 I used AI to code a personal trainer app in one weekend. I see why big apps fear the competition.
👉 A former group fitness coach built a personal trainer app in one weekend using AI tool Manus, requiring minimal coding experience and no prior knowledge of programming. The app generates workout programs, logs lifts, and provides exercise cues, demonstrating the potential for individuals to create their own apps without relying on large technology companies.

📰 Google plans to let software engineers use AI assistants in job interviews
👉 Google is piloting a new interview process for junior-to-mid-level software engineering candidates, allowing them to use an approved AI assistant during the "code comprehension" round. The AI assistant will be used to evaluate candidates' fluency, including prompt engineering, output validation, and debugging skills. The pilot program aims to better align with the modern engineering landscape and is set to roll out more widely across the company if successful.

📰 SpaceX strikes $60 billion deal for the right to buy coding startup Cursor
👉 SpaceX has agreed to buy coding startup Cursor for $60 billion or pay $10 billion for the work it's doing. The partnership gives SpaceX access to Cursor's leading product and distribution to expert software engineers, while also providing Cursor with access to SpaceX's computing resources, including its supercomputer Colossus.

📰 An early-stage VC shares why he's not investing in AI coding startups — but doubling down on these founders
👉 Jussi Salovaara, co-founder and managing partner at Antler, a venture capital firm, has decided not to invest in AI-assisted coding startups due to the already crowded market. Instead, he is focusing on founders who are using AI to improve their code-writing efficiency and tool-building capabilities.

📰 ChatGPT 5.5 Is All About Math, Science and AI Research
👉 OpenAI releases ChatGPT 5.5, a new family of models designed for coding, computer use, and research. The model is more intuitive than previous versions and can independently complete tasks, making it useful for people doing intensive work like coding or research. It also features agentic capabilities and scored higher on benchmarks for math problem-solving and app usage.

📰 Vibe coding and agentic engineering are getting closer than I'd like
👉 The author of the article has noticed a convergence between vibe coding and agentic engineering in their own work, blurring the distinction they previously made between the two concepts. Vibe coding involves using AI to generate code without understanding how it works, while agentic engineering uses AI to assist programming with human oversight.

📰 KPMG now has a dashboard where its consultants can track how often their colleagues use AI
👉 KPMG has introduced a dashboard for its US advisory division, allowing consultants to track their use of artificial intelligence (AI) and compare it to their target goals and peer group. The firm aims to encourage more frequent and sophisticated AI use among its 10,000 employees, as data shows that regular AI users produce higher-quality work and spend more time on strategic tasks.

📰 China’s DeepSeek previews new AI model a year after jolting US rivals
👉 Chinese AI company DeepSeek previewed its next-generation model, V4, claiming it can compete with leading closed-source systems from US rivals like Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI. V4 marks a major improvement over prior models, particularly in coding capabilities. The release is seen as a milestone for China's chip industry, with compatibility with domestic Huawei technology highlighted.

📰 A looming crisis could limit some of your favorite AI tools
👉 Microsoft's GitHub Copilot has paused new signups for some plans and tightened usage limits, while Anthropic is testing restrictions on its popular AI tool Claude Code due to overwhelming demand. These moves are warning signs that the availability of certain AI tools may become limited as companies struggle to keep up with increasing user adoption and compute demands.

📰 Forget tracking AI use. Goldman's tech boss cares about this instead.
👉 Goldman Sachs' Chief Information Officer Marco Argenti is prioritizing team performance over individual AI use metrics, focusing on evaluating teams' velocity with AI tools rather than tracking employee activity. The firm's goal is to speed up project timelines, improve quality control, and track AI token consumption for budgeting, shifting away from the trend of monitoring individual users' AI-related activities.
